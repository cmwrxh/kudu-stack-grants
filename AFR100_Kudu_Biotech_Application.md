## SECTION B: PROJECT SUMMARY (250 words)

Kudu Biotech proposes to deploy the Kudu Intelligent Ecosystem Stack (KIES) — an edge-AI wildlife telemetry and carbon sequestration monitoring platform — in Kenyan conservancies participating in the AFR100 landscape restoration initiative.

The problem: AFR100 commitments require proof of restoration outcomes, but current monitoring relies on manual surveys and satellite imagery with 6–12 month lag times. Conservancies cannot prove biomass growth, detect deforestation early, or verify carbon credits in real time.

Our solution: KIES deploys solar-powered sensor nodes with multispectral cameras and IoT sensors to monitor biomass growth continuously. Edge AI processes data locally, issuing "Proof of Growth" certificates on an immutable Rust ledger. If deforestation is detected, carbon credit payouts pause instantly — protecting both buyers and local communities.

This project directly supports AFR100's goal of 100 million hectares of restored landscape in Africa by 2030. We target the Loita Hills-Maasai Mara buffer zone, a critical wildlife corridor under restoration pressure.

Innovation: Unlike foreign-built platforms (Pachama, OpenForests), KIES is engineered in Nairobi for African infrastructure realities — unreliable power, minimal connectivity, and $5 servers. Our AlphaCore engine provides 15x better performance than standard solutions on the same hardware.

Outcomes: 10 sensor nodes deployed, 5,000 hectares under active monitoring, first verified carbon credits within 9 months, and a replicable model for other AFR100 landscapes.

## SECTION C: PROBLEM STATEMENT

### C.1 Landscape Context

The Loita Hills-Maasai Mara ecosystem spans 150,000 hectares of critical wildlife corridor. It is:
- A top-5 carbon sequestration opportunity in Kenya (UN-REDD assessment)
- Home to 30+ endangered species, including 400+ elephants
- Under threat from agricultural encroachment, charcoal production, and drought
- Managed by 5 community conservancies with limited technical capacity

### C.2 Monitoring Gap

Current AFR100 monitoring in this landscape relies on:
- **Annual ground surveys:** 3-month lag, $15,000 per survey, covers <5% of area
- **Satellite imagery (Landsat):** 30m resolution, 16-day revisit, cloud contamination
- **Manual carbon accounting:** Excel-based, vulnerable to error and fraud

**Result:** Conservancies cannot prove restoration outcomes to carbon buyers. Funding is delayed or denied. Communities lose income. Restoration stalls.

### C.3 Why This Matters for AFR100

AFR100 requires countries to report on restoration progress. Kenya's commitment is 5.1 million hectares. Without local monitoring infrastructure, reporting will be:
- Expensive (reliance on foreign consultants)
- Slow (annual or bi-annual updates)
- Untrusted (manual data, no cryptographic proof)

# PROBLEM STATEMENT
## The Trust Gap in African Conservation & Energy

---

## 1. THE CARBON MARKET IS BROKEN

**The Promise:** Pay developing countries to protect forests. Rich nations offset emissions. Everyone wins.

**The Reality:**
- 30% of carbon credits may be worthless or fraudulent (Guardian/Climate Home investigation, 2023)
- Verification relies on manual site visits — expensive, infrequent, gameable
- Deforestation often happens *between* audits
- The EU Green Transition Directive (2026) now criminalizes false claims — buyers are terrified

**The African Angle:**
- Kenya is a top-10 carbon credit issuer globally
- But local communities see little benefit; middlemen capture most value
- No technical infrastructure exists to prove conservation outcomes locally

---

## 2. BIODIVERSITY FUNDING IS BLIND

**The Promise:** International sponsors fund endangered species protection.

**The Reality:**
- Funding is released based on annual census data — 12 months old
- Poaching events discovered days or weeks after they happen
- Rangers patrol on foot with radios — reactive, not predictive
- "Proof of Life" for sponsors means a PDF report, not real data

**The African Angle:**
- Kenya loses 100+ elephants per year to poaching
- Rhino populations are recovering but fragile
- 60% of wildlife lives outside national parks, in underfunded conservancies

---

## 3. WATER AND ENERGY ARE SILOED

**The Promise:** Smart water management and clean energy will solve scarcity.

**The Reality:**
- Kenyan water utilities lose 30–40% of supply to leaks and theft
- Desalination and smart irrigation are energy-intensive — but no one models the trade-off
- Mini-grid operators lack predictive demand tools
- E-waste from solar batteries is becoming a crisis with no tracking system

**The African Angle:**
- UN declared 2026 the "era of global water bankruptcy"
- Kenya's arid counties (Turkana, Marsabit, Wajir) face existential water risk
- EEP Africa's 2026 Kenya mandate is specifically e-waste from clean energy

---

## 4. THE INFRASTRUCTURE GAP

**The Promise:** Technology will save conservation.

**The Reality:**
- Most conservation tech is built in San Francisco or Berlin for San Francisco or Berlin
- It assumes fiber internet, stable power, and $500/cloud instances
- It fails in Maasai Mara, where power flickers and 3G is a luxury
- Local engineers are hired as implementers, not architects

**The African Angle:**
- Kenya has world-class engineering talent (Andela, Moringa, iHub alumni)
- But conservation tech procurement goes to foreign vendors
- Sovereign engineering — built locally, for local conditions — is rare

---

## THE COMPOUND EFFECT

These four gaps reinforce each other:

```
No verification → No trust → No funding → No conservation → No water → No energy → No verification
```

**Kudu breaks this cycle by building trust infrastructure at the edge.**
